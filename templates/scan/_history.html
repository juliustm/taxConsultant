{# The field app's home screen: what this device has captured, and what became of it. Two layers. A hero pinned to the viewport answers the question someone opens the app holding - how much have I captured this month, and is anything wrong - in figures big enough to read at arm's length. Below it a sheet of detail slides up over the hero as it is scrolled, carrying the two actions that matter (refresh, Scan) in a header that sticks to the top once it gets there. The movement is the platform's own scroller and nothing else; see .dash in static/css/scan.css for why that matters on a cheap phone. The list itself reads entirely from IndexedDB, so it looks the same with or without a network - the pull from the server just replaces what is cached. Two lists are merged into one view: receipts still waiting on the phone, and receipts the server has. Without the first half a field user has no way to tell "not sent yet" from "lost". #} {# x-show rather than x-if so a list scrolled halfway down, its search query and its expanded row all survive a trip to the scanner and back. The cost of keeping it mounted is a hidden subtree; the cost of not doing is re-reading IndexedDB and losing the user's place every time they check a code against the receipt in hand. #}
